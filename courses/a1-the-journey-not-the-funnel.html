The journey, not the funnel

Your dashboards show a funnel: 360,000 sessions in at the top, 9,720 orders out at the bottom, gates in between. But no shopper has ever walked through a funnel. They open an app, search for a linen dress, read reviews, leave, come back. This session retrains your eye: a session is a story, and the funnel is that story with everything interesting deleted. Part 1 · one session, two renderings

A session is a story 15 min live

Meet Maya, Tuesday evening. She opens the Mango Lane app, types "linen dress" into search, opens one dress, reads the reviews, adds size M to her cart, and buys. Six minutes, start to finish. Your funnel report records this as four green ticks: reached PDP, reached cart, reached checkout, purchased. True - and empty. The funnel keeps the gates and deletes the walk: where she entered, which road she took, what she read before she trusted the product.

LiveWhy the walk matters more than the gates5 min▶

Every fact the funnel deleted is a lever you own:

  • Where she entered. Maya started at Home. Half of Mango Lane's sessions do not - and where a session starts largely decides how it ends (a2 is entirely about this).
  • Which road she took. Maya searched. Searchers and browsers reach the same PDP gate but convert very differently - the search road produces orders at 2.67x the browse road's rate here (a3's whole story).
  • What she did before trusting the product. She read reviews before adding to cart. That in-page behavior is where PDP conversion is actually won or lost (a4).
Real world

This is not a metaphor - it is a report. GA4's path exploration draws exactly this: a tree of the screens and events sessions actually walked, forward from any entry or backward from purchase ("how did buyers get here?"). Your analysts can pull it today. The leader skill is knowing it exists and asking for it by name.

What to ask "Show me one real session as a story, not a funnel row - entry, road, and what they did on the product page." If your team cannot narrate a single session, they are managing gates blind.
Self-studyThe funnel is still useful - as a summary2 min read▶

Nothing here retires the funnel. Gates are how you track the health of the whole system month over month, and the prequel course taught you to decompose them. The reframe is about diagnosis: the funnel tells you WHERE the number dropped; only the journey tells you WHY. When a gate moves, the next question is never "which gate" (you already know) - it is "which door, which road, which behavior changed". That question order is this entire track in one sentence.

Part 2 · three systematic blind spots

What the funnel hides 12 min live

The funnel does not hide things randomly - it hides the same three things every time, because its shape assumes every session starts at the top, walks one road, and intended to buy. At Mango Lane, none of those assumptions hold.

LiveBlind spot 1 · multiple doors4 min▶

The funnel draws one entrance at the top. Mango Lane has five. Only half of sessions start at Home - and 54,000 sessions a month land straight on a product page from an ad, a push notification, or a link a friend shared. Those sessions never see your homepage, your hero banner, or your carefully merchandised categories. For them, the landing PDP IS the brand's front door.

What to ask "What share of our sessions start somewhere other than Home - and who owns the experience of each door?" If the answer is "the homepage team owns landing", 15% of your store has no owner.
LiveBlind spot 2 · multiple roads4 min▶

The funnel's "reached PDP" gate merges two very different shoppers: the one who typed what she wanted into search, and the one who wandered the category grids. Same gate, different intent, different economics - at Mango Lane the search road converts at 2.67x the browse road. a3 unpacks this properly; for now, hold the shape: a single gate number is always an average across roads, and averages across roads are where diagnoses go to die.

What to ask "When you show me PDP conversion, split it by road - search versus browse. Which road moved?" One question, and a flat average becomes a diagnosis.
LiveBlind spot 3 · dead zones4 min▶

36,000 sessions a month land on account, order-tracking, and support screens. They check a delivery date and leave. They were never going to buy - and that is fine; they are customers being served. But they sit inside the 360,000 denominator of your site conversion rate, quietly dragging it down. A store whose service traffic grows looks like a store whose shopping is getting worse, unless someone separates the two.

What to ask "What share of our sessions were never shopping sessions - and is our conversion rate quoted with or without them?" You are not asking to inflate the number; you are asking to stop mixing two businesses in one ratio.
Demo · read along, no computation

Three sessions, told twice each ★ 10 min · read-along

Three real journey shapes from Mango Lane's month. For each: what the funnel would say, then what the journey shows. Notice how often the funnel's verdict is not just incomplete - it is wrong.

LiveStory 1 · the 90-second buyer3 min▶

The walk. A push notification ("Your size is back in stock") deep-links straight to a PDP. She lands, confirms the size, taps buy. 90 seconds, no Home, no search, no browsing.

The funnel says. A perfect four-tick conversion - indistinguishable from Maya's six-minute considered purchase.

The journey shows. A manufactured door working exactly as designed: right message, right person, landing screen matched the promise. This session's success was decided before it started - by whoever wrote the push and chose the deeplink.

LiveStory 2 · the researcher who "failed"3 min▶

The walk. Home, into the dresses category, then six PDPs in a row - photos, reviews, size guides - then leaves without adding anything to cart.

The funnel says. Abandoned at the PDP-to-cart gate. A failure. One of the 108,000 PDP sessions that did not cart.

The journey shows. Healthy research behavior - a shopper building a shortlist, very likely to return and buy on a later session. Punishing this session with a "fix the PDP" project would optimize against your own best customers mid-decision.

LiveStory 3 · the invisible leak3 min▶

The walk. She searches "petite linen blazer". Zero results. She exits. Eleven seconds.

The funnel says. Almost nothing - a session that never reached the first gate. It dissolves into the 216,000 sessions that never saw a PDP, indistinguishable from idle window-shopping.

The journey shows. The highest-intent visitor of the three - she told you exactly what she wanted, in her own words - lost to an empty results screen. Zero-results searches are a demand signal AND a fixable leak, and the funnel files them under "nothing happened".

What to ask "What are our top ten zero-result search terms this month?" It is one of the cheapest reports in analytics and one of the most commonly never-pulled.
Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 15 min total

  • Watch three real sessions - in your own store's session-replay tool if you have one, or simply over a colleague's shoulder in any shopping app.
  • Write each one as a one-line story: door, road, what they did, how it ended. ("Push to PDP, checked size, bought in 90 seconds.")
  • For each story, note what your funnel report would have said about it - and whether that verdict was fair.
  • Bring your best one-liner to session a2. The doors those three sessions used are exactly where we go next.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The funnel and the journey disagree most about the browse session (Home, six PDPs, no cart). Who is right?

Research sessions are how considered purchases start. The funnel scores them as failures; the journey reads intent building. Fixing what is not broken is the classic funnel-only mistake.

2 · 54,000 sessions land straight on a product page. What does the classic funnel picture assume that they break?

The funnel draws one entrance. Mango Lane has five doors, and the PDP-direct door delivers 54,000 sessions past the "top" without ever seeing it - a2 is entirely about those doors.

3 · Why is the zero-results search exit called the invisible leak?

The session told you exactly what she wanted and got an empty screen - but no funnel gate moved, so no dashboard flags it. The zero-results report exists; someone has to ask for it.
